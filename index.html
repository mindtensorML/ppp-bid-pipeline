PPP Bid Preparation Process

The bidder's mirror of the procuring authority's tender, mapped against the APMG PPP Certification Guide.

APMG PPP Certification Guide (ADB, EBRD, IDB, IsDB and WBG, 2016)Built at SpaceXAI and shared with permissionA general method with no client data

The guide is written for the procuring authority and covers the bidder mainly in its Chapter 6 appendix. This map follows the bid from the first pipeline signal to financial close and the handover to delivery. Each phase names the authority step it answers. Each task cites the guide where the guide speaks to it. Where it does not, the step is marked as bidder practice.

Who signs

Every task and gate has a named owner. Binding offers, commitments to lenders, contractors, insurers and banks, and every legal and financial sign-off stay with people. Where a model drafts, the owner reviews and signs. Authority parameters such as the tender route and revenue regime are read from the tender documents, never chosen by the bidder. Figures quoted from the guide are its benchmarks, and the RFP governs.

Phase 00

Origination and Pre-Tender Engagement

Procuring authority stepThe procuring authority builds its pipeline, screens and appraises the project, and sounds the market before launch.
Guide anchors · Ch 3 s3, s11, Box 3.14; Ch 4 s9; Ch 5 s3.9, s3.14, s6; Ch 2 s1.6.6
  1. OR-D0Decision gateLead sponsor

    Opportunity origin?

    An authority tender, our own unsolicited proposal, or a challenge to another party's proposal.

    Ch 2 pp.54-57; Ch 3 p.11
  2. OR-01Model-runBusiness development lead

    Monitor pipelines, plans and pre-tender signals

    Track published pipelines and schedules, advisor appointments, preparation-facility support and consultation records. Pipeline inclusion marks a potential PPP candidate. The green light to procure comes only after appraisal.

    Ch 3 p.8, pp.9-10, p.35, Box 3.14 p.45, s12 pp.37-40; Ch 4 s1 p.7
  3. OR-02Model-runBusiness development lead

    Review the Project Information Memorandum and published studies

    Read the PIM, industry-day material and any draft documents released before launch.

    Ch 5 s6 pp.105-107, s3.9 p.24; Ch 4 s9.1 p.48
  4. OR-03Human-onlyLead sponsor

    Respond to market sounding

    Give candid views on scope, risk allocation and bankability. Participation gives no advantage and is not a bid or an expression of interest.

    Ch 4 s9 pp.47-52; Ch 3 s12 p.39
  5. OR-04Human-onlyBid manager

    Take part in pre-tender consultation

    Join pre-tender meetings and comment on draft documents in writing. Data-room access before launch is under NDA protocols. Keeping a log of every exchange is bidder practice.

    Ch 5 s3.14 p.27

Phase 01

Opportunity Screening and Decision to Participate

Procuring authority stepTender launch. The authority announces the project and issues the RFQ, EOI call or RFP.
Guide anchors · Ch 6 s4, s6.1, App A s6.1-6.3; Ch 1 Table 1.7, Box 1.36; Ch 2 pp.16-35, p.70; Ch 3 s10
  1. OS-01HybridBid manager

    Receive tender notice and obtain the documents

    Register as required, pay any document fee and log the timetable.

    Ch 6 s6.1 p.17
  2. OS-P1Authority parameterProcuring authority

    Tender route

    Read from the tender documents. One-stage open, two-stage with pass or fail pre-qualification, restricted with a short list, competitive dialogue or interaction, or negotiated with BAFO. It decides whether a separate qualification stage and a dialogue happen.

    Ch 1 Box 1.36 p.152; Ch 4 App A pp.101-109; Ch 5 Table 5.5 pp.108-110
  3. OS-02HybridBid manager

    Verify green light and approvals status

    Confirm approval to tender, permit and land status, and any pending treasury, cabinet or legislative approvals.

    Ch 4 s1 p.7, s13.1 p.64; Ch 2 p.49, s1.7.5 p.70
  4. OS-03HybridLegal counsel

    Screen the country PPP framework

    The authority's power to contract, procurement law, scope limits, currency convertibility and repatriation, tax and land rules.

    Ch 2 pp.16-21, p.32, pp.34-35
  5. OS-04HybridFinancial lead

    Check affordability and payment source

    For government-pays or mixed deals, test budget appropriation, PPP commitment limits and counterparty credit.

    Ch 3 pp.33-34; Ch 2 p.86, pp.92-93
  6. OS-05HybridLead sponsor

    Screen market fit and capacity to qualify

    The guide's acceptability conditions, precedent transactions and likely rivals, and whether we can meet the qualification criteria alone.

    Ch 6 App A s6.2.2 p.50, Box 6A.1 p.52; Ch 1 Table 1.7 p.130; Ch 3 p.16, p.33
  7. OS-06HybridBid manager

    Preliminary risk assessment

    Constraints and dependencies, authority preparedness and political support, and our own check of the authority's demand forecast.

    Ch 3 s7 p.15, Box 3.6 p.17; Ch 6 App A s6.3 pp.53-54
  8. OS-07HybridFinancial lead

    Estimate bid costs

    Advisers, surveys, the bid bond, any stipend, and any development-fund fee the winner repays at contract signing.

    Ch 6 App A s6.2.2 p.50, Box 6A.1 p.52; Ch 3 p.43 fn 36, Box 3.14 p.45; Ch 4 s6.2 p.26
  9. OS-08HybridBid manager

    Project Evaluation Memo (PEM)

    Brings the screens, risks and costs together for the decision to participate.

    Ch 6 App A Fig 6A.3 p.48
  10. OS-D1Decision gateLead sponsor

    Decision to participate?

    Participate, gather more information, or reject. Resources are spent only after a positive decision.

    Ch 6 App A s6.1 p.41, s6.2.2 p.50

Phase 02

Consortium, Governance and Qualification

Procuring authority stepQualification. The authority assesses the Submission of Qualifications pass or fail, or short-lists.
Guide anchors · Ch 6 s7, App A s6.3-6.5; Ch 5 s7; Ch 1 Box 1.21 p.89, p.116
  1. CQ-D1Decision gateLead sponsor

    Consortium required?

    A consortium is the usual bidder. A single bidder moves straight to governance.

    Ch 6 App A s6.4.1 p.57; Gl p.10
  2. CQ-01Human-onlyLead sponsor

    NDA, letter of intent and heads of terms with partners

    Exclusivity, roles and cost sharing, agreed before qualification work starts.

    Ch 6 App A s6.4.2 pp.59-60; Gl p.23, p.27
  3. CQ-02Human-onlySponsors

    Execute the consortium agreement

    Shareholdings as committed at bid submission, voting and deadlock rules, cost sharing, and share lock-up.

    Ch 1 Box 1.21 p.89, p.116; Ch 6 App A s6.4.2 pp.59-60, s6.4.3 p.63, Table 6A.2 p.89; Ch 7 s8.2 p.63
  4. CQ-03Human-onlySteering committee

    Steering committee and decision-rights matrix

    Each partner confirms its decision to participate. The committee sets risk thresholds and directs the bid manager the consortium appoints. Voting, reserved matters and deadlock rules are agreed. The decision-rights matrix is bidder practice.

    Ch 6 App A s6.3 pp.52-54, s6.4.3 pp.61-63, s6.7 p.70; Bidder practice
  5. CQ-04Human-onlyBid manager

    Appoint advisers with conflict checks

    Legal, technical, financial and local advisers. Check conflicts and set information barriers. Insurance advice is bidder practice.

    Ch 6 App A s6.5 pp.64-67
  6. CQ-05HybridLegal counsel

    Probity and anti-corruption rules

    Corrupt conduct can lead the authority to call the bid bond, cancel the preferred-bidder appointment or contract, and sue for damages.

    Ch 2 pp.103-104; Ch 6 s14 p.36
  7. CQ-06Human-onlyLegal counsel

    Confirm NDA and data-room terms allow AI processing

    Many NDAs and data-room rules restrict copying or third-party processing. No G or Y task touches NDA-protected or data-room material until this is cleared.

    Bidder practice; Ch 5 s3.14 p.27
  8. CQ-07HybridBid manager

    Prepare the Submission of Qualifications (SoQ)

    Legal, financial-economic and technical credentials of the consortium, with evidence of the consortium agreement.

    Ch 5 s7 pp.110-115; Gl p.48
  9. CQ-08Human-onlyBid manager

    Submit the SoQ

    After qualification, consortium changes are allowed only as the RFQ permits. Replacing or removing members is generally not allowed except in exceptional cases.

    Ch 6 Fig 6.2 p.12, s7 pp.21-22; Ch 5 s7.4 p.118
  10. CQ-D2Decision gateProcuring authority

    Qualified or short-listed?

    Pass or fail pre-qualification, or a short list. If not qualified, archive and ask for feedback where offered.

    Ch 5 s7.4 pp.117-118; Ch 6 s7 pp.21-22
  11. CQ-09HybridBid manager

    Carry qualification documents into the RFP response

    One-stage route. Qualifications are submitted with the proposal.

    Ch 6 s4 p.11; Ch 4 App A p.102

Phase 03

Bid Period Mobilisation and Strategy

Procuring authority stepBid period. The authority issues the RFP or invitation to propose, opens the data room, holds the conference and site visits, and answers clarifications.
Guide anchors · Ch 6 s5, s6.2-6.7, App A s6.7; Ch 5 s8.1-8.2, Box 5.17; Ch 4 s16.2.2
  1. BM-01HybridBid manager

    Receive the RFP or ITP and the draft PPP contract

    Submission rules, proposal validity period, evaluation basis, draft contract, schedules and bid bond terms. Legal confirms which obligations can be accepted.

    Ch 6 App A s6.7.3 p.86; Ch 1 p.153; Ch 5 s8.1 p.122
  2. BM-02HybridLegal counsel

    Open the living compliance matrix

    One matrix maps every RFP requirement to its owner and location. It runs from here to submission.

    Bidder practice; Ch 6 s9.1 p.25
  3. BM-03HybridTechnical lead

    Data room review and bidder due diligence

    The authority does not normally warrant its technical information, so each bidder obtains its own studies. Log the reliance status of each document.

    Ch 6 s6.2 p.19, App A s6.7.1 pp.73-74; Ch 5 s3.14 p.27
  4. BM-04Human-onlyBid manager

    Bidders' conference and site visits

    Attend, record what was said, and turn open points into written questions.

    Ch 6 s6.2 p.19, Box 6.2 p.10
  5. BM-05HybridBid manager

    Analyse the evaluation criteria

    Price-only with technical pass or fail, or price and quality (MEAT, QCBS). Note minimum technical scores and envelope order.

    Ch 5 s8.2 pp.125-133; Gl p.38
  6. BM-06HybridBid manager

    Clarification loop to the question deadline

    Questions come from every workstream. Answers usually go to all bidders and do not override the RFP unless it is formally amended. Re-baseline the workstreams after each addendum.

    Ch 6 s6.3-6.4 pp.19-20, fn 6
  7. BM-07HybridBid manager

    Competitive intelligence and authority reference price

    Likely rivals, precedent deals, and an estimate of the authority's shadow bid or published reference price.

    Ch 4 s6 p.23 fn 7, s6.5 p.31; Ch 3 p.16, p.33
  8. BM-08HybridFinancial lead

    Price the authority's risk matrix

    The authority allocates risk. For each transferred risk, decide whether to price it, pass it back-to-back or insure it. The all-risk clause leaves unnamed risks with the private party.

    Ch 5 Box 5.17 pp.83-84, s5.9 p.104; Ch 4 s5.2 p.22
  9. BM-09Human-onlySteering committee

    Bid strategy and win themes

    Anchor themes to the evaluation criteria and the authority's value-for-money drivers. The steering committee approves the strategy.

    Ch 6 App A s6.4.3 p.62; Ch 4 s16.2.2 pp.83-84
  10. BM-10HybridBid manager

    Bid plan, budget and approval calendar

    Staff the reviews, request the bid bond facility early, and fix sponsor investment committee dates. The guide says 90 to 120 days may be preferable for open tenders (30 to 90 is common). Two-stage processes that require extensive designs and committed finance need longer, typically about 150 days in Australia.

    Ch 6 App A s6.7 p.70, s6.4.3 p.63; Ch 6 s5 p.15; Ch 5 s8.1 p.122

Competitive Dialogue or Interaction

Procuring authority stepThe authority holds dialogue or one-to-one sessions with short-listed bidders, then closes the dialogue and issues the final tender (ITP, ITSFT).
Guide anchors · Ch 5 s8.3, Box 5.27; Ch 6 s8-8.1; Ch 4 App A pp.104-105
  1. DL-01Human-onlyBid manager

    Dialogue meetings and interim submissions

    Interim submissions come from the workstreams' current drafts. In EU dialogue the sessions shape the RFP and contract. In AU and NZ interaction they shape the bids.

    Ch 5 s8.3 pp.134-136; Ch 6 s8-8.1 pp.22-24
  2. DL-02HybridBid manager

    Business solutions and confidential ideas

    Propose solutions and mark what is confidential to us.

    Ch 5 s8.3 p.136
  3. DL-03HybridFinancial lead

    Stipend and dialogue bond terms

    Record any stipend payable to responsive bidders (capped) and any dialogue bond required.

    Ch 5 s8.3 p.137
  4. DL-04HybridBid manager

    Final tender after dialogue closes (ITP or ITSFT)

    Re-baseline every workstream and the compliance matrix to the final documents.

    Ch 1 p.154; Ch 5 s8.3 p.134

Phase 04

Technical Workstream

Procuring authority stepThe authority's output specifications, design approach, technical studies and E&S assessments are in the tender documents.
Guide anchors · Ch 4 s4, s13-14; Ch 6 App A s6.7.1; Ch 7 s4-7; Ch 8 s1, s3, s11
  1. TW-01HybridTechnical lead

    Extract output specifications, performance and hand-back requirements

    Specs define results, not means. A person certifies the extract is complete against the RFP index.

    Ch 4 s4.2.2 p.14; Ch 8 s4.2 p.18; Gl p.31
  2. TW-02HybridTechnical lead

    Review the authority's technical studies and site data

    Geotechnical, utilities, land, resettlement and archaeology. Record what the authority warrants and what we must verify.

    Ch 4 s4.2.3 p.15, s7 pp.36-37; Ch 6 App A s6.7.1 pp.73-74
  3. TW-P1Authority parameterProcuring authority

    Design approach

    Functional, reference or full design. With full design some design risk stays with the authority.

    Ch 4 s4.2.1 pp.13-14; Ch 6 App A p.74; Gl p.41
  4. TW-03HybridTechnical lead

    Design proposal to output specification

    Bid design is conceptual and the final design is developed after award. The guide's good practice is that the authority reviews but does not approve design. Check the draft contract, since some include an approval to proceed.

    Ch 7 s6.1.2 pp.46-47, s7.3 p.55; Ch 6 App A s6.7.1 p.74
  5. TW-04HybridTechnical lead

    O&M and whole-life strategy, run with design

    Higher specifications cost more to build and less to run. The steering committee settles the trade-off so whole-life cost is minimised.

    Ch 8 s1.1 p.6; Ch 6 App A s6.7.1 p.75
  6. TW-05HybridTechnical lead

    Life-cycle, renewal and hand-back plan

    Major maintenance and renewals by year, residual-life targets, final survey and hand-back security. Feeds the cost base.

    Ch 8 s3.1.5 p.15, s5.4 pp.27-28, s11 pp.46-48; Ch 5 s9.10 p.161
  7. TW-06HybridTechnical lead

    Construction methodology and baseline schedule

    Method statements, quality plan and critical path. The guide expects them finalised by the end of the tender phase, to serve as the baseline for monitoring.

    Ch 7 s4.1 p.14, Table 7.7 p.58
  8. TW-P2Authority parameterProcuring authority

    E&S approval status

    Full approval, provisional permit, or a roadmap to approval. The assessment itself is never skipped.

    Ch 4 s13.1-13.2 p.64, p.15
  9. TW-07HybridTechnical lead

    E&S review against the authority's EIA and lender standards

    An environmental assessment applies to every project. Close gaps to the Equator Principles and MDB standards and price the mitigation.

    Ch 4 p.15, s13 pp.61-65
  10. TW-08HybridTechnical lead

    Social action plan and health and safety plan

    Adopt the authority's social baseline and price the private-sector actions. Health and safety plans are common evaluation criteria.

    Ch 4 s14 pp.65-70; Ch 5 s8.2 pp.130-131
  11. TW-09Human-onlyTechnical lead

    Key personnel

    Name key personnel where the RFP requires it. The contract will require suitably experienced and qualified staff.

    Ch 7 s4.2.1 p.19; Ch 5 s8.2 p.131
  12. TW-10HybridTechnical lead

    First full draft of the technical offer

    Draft for compliance review. Sign-off comes at submission.

    Ch 5 s8.1 p.123

Phase 05

Financial Workstream

Procuring authority stepThe authority fixes the revenue regime, payment mechanism, bid variable, model template, financing evidence rule and any payment ceiling or fee floor.
Guide anchors · Ch 4 s5-6, s8; Ch 5 s3.8, s4, s8.1-8.2, s9.5, s9.9; Ch 6 App A s6.6-6.8; Ch 1 s7; Ch 8 s5, s7.8
  1. FW-01HybridFinancial lead

    Extract financial requirements

    Model template and mandated assumptions, minimum equity, maximum leverage, the financing evidence rule, and any ceiling or floor.

    Ch 5 s8.1 p.124, s3.8 p.24; Ch 1 p.104, p.115
  2. FW-P1Authority parameterProcuring authority

    Revenue regime

    User-pays, government-pays or mixed. Set by the authority.

    Ch 4 s5.1 pp.19-20; Ch 5 s4.7 pp.50-51; Gl p.22, p.51
  3. FW-02HybridFinancial lead

    User-pays revenue model

    Our own demand forecast with sensitivities, elasticity, tariff caps, ancillary revenue and revenue sharing.

    Ch 3 Box 3.6 p.17; Ch 4 s6.5 pp.30-31; Ch 5 s4.8 pp.52-53
  4. FW-03HybridFinancial lead

    Government-pays model

    Availability payments or volume-linked payments, indexation, payment from service commencement and phased ramp-up.

    Ch 5 s4.9-4.10 pp.55-72; Ch 8 pp.22-23
  5. FW-04HybridFinancial lead

    Mixed model with public contribution

    User charges plus grant, viability gap funding or a minimum revenue guarantee.

    Ch 5 s4.2-4.6 pp.37-50
  6. FW-05HybridFinancial lead

    Payment mechanism and deduction exposure

    Dry-run the mechanism for deductions, ratchets, performance points and relief events. The guide's position is that non-availability deductions should generally be uncapped, though EMDE contracts may cap them and performance deductions are often capped. Read the RFP.

    Ch 5 Box 5.13 p.63, s9.3 pp.144-146, s5.7 p.97; Ch 8 s4.3 pp.19-20, p.23; Ch 6 App A p.78
  7. FW-06Human-onlySteering committee

    Contractor and operator priced offers

    Fixed-price, date-certain construction offer and O&M offer on back-to-back heads of terms. Where a sponsor is also the contractor, manage the conflict of interest over price and over which project agreement terms the SPV accepts.

    Ch 6 App A p.57, p.69, p.78, s6.7.3 pp.91-93
  8. FW-07HybridFinancial lead

    Risk-adjusted Capex, Opex and life-cycle costs

    Interest during construction, lender and adviser fees, insurance, bonds, permits, E&S measures, the renewal fund and hand-back security. Over-aggressive bidding is the first common cause of cost overruns the guide lists.

    Ch 4 s4.4 p.16, s6.2-6.3 pp.25-28; Ch 8 s5.4 p.27, p.28; Ch 7 s5.3 pp.43-44
  9. FW-08HybridFinancial lead

    Tax and SPV structuring

    Withholding tax, VAT on construction, thin capitalisation and holding structure.

    Bidder practice
  10. FW-09HybridFinancial lead

    Currency and hedging strategy

    Revenue and debt currency mismatch, convertibility, and the interest-rate and FX hedging plan to close.

    Ch 2 pp.34-35; Ch 1 pp.78-79, p.105, p.113, p.118
  11. FW-P3Authority parameterProcuring authority

    Financing evidence required at bid

    Committed debt, common in dialogue and short-listed routes, or indicative support letters, common in open tenders.

    Ch 1 p.104, pp.117-118; Ch 6 s5 p.15
  12. FW-12HybridFinancial lead

    Lender engagement and funding competition

    Information memorandum to lenders, indicative terms, term sheet and mandate letter.

    Ch 6 App A s6.8.1 pp.94-95
  13. FW-P2Authority parameterProcuring authority

    Bid variable

    Availability payment, grant or subsidy, concession fee, tariff, or term. The model solves for it.

    Ch 5 s8.2 pp.125-127; Ch 4 s6.5 p.31, s8.4 p.46
  14. FW-10HybridFinancial lead

    Build the base case financial model

    The winning model becomes the contract base case for variations, compensation, refinancing gain and termination sums.

    Ch 8 s5.2 p.24; Ch 5 Box 5.34 p.148, s9.9 p.157; Ch 7 s9.4 p.77
  15. FW-11HybridFinancial lead

    Scenarios, cover ratios and equity return

    Construction delay with LDs, deductions, prolonged force majeure and termination cases. DSCR, LLCR and PLCR against lender thresholds, and equity IRR against the hurdle rate.

    Ch 4 s6.9 p.34, s8.1.1-8.1.2 pp.38-44; Ch 7 s10.2 pp.80-81; Ch 8 s5.3 pp.25-27, s9.2-9.4 pp.42-44
  16. FW-13HybridFinancial lead

    Support lenders' due diligence

    Technical, legal and model due diligence, and running and life-cycle costs.

    Ch 1 p.97, App A pp.169-170; Ch 6 App A s6.8.2 p.95, s6.8.4.1 p.102
  17. FW-14HybridFinancial lead

    Bid bond, guarantees and insurance plan

    Guide benchmarks are a bid bond around 1 percent of the value of the project and a performance guarantee around 2 to 4 percent of Capex. Add parent guarantees, insurance and political risk cover. The RFP governs.

    Ch 5 s8.1 p.124, s9.5 pp.149-150; Ch 1 p.104, p.106
  18. FW-15HybridFinancial lead

    Refinancing gain-share position

    Read the RFP's gain-share formula. A refinancing fully reflected in the approved base case falls outside the approval regime.

    Ch 8 s7.8 pp.36-39; Ch 1 p.117
  19. FW-D1Decision gateFinancial lead

    Price within the ceiling or above the fee floor?

    A bid outside the RFP's maximum or minimum fails. If outside, rework the model.

    Ch 5 s3.8 p.24; Ch 4 s6.5 p.31
  20. FW-16HybridFinancial lead

    First full draft of the financial offer

    Price appears only in the financial envelope.

    Ch 5 s8.1 p.123 fn 74

Phase 06

Legal Workstream

Procuring authority stepThe authority's draft PPP contract sets risk allocation, relief and compensation events, termination, step-in and disputes.
Guide anchors · Ch 5 s5.7-5.9, s9, App A; Ch 2 pp.19-35; Ch 6 App A s6.7.3; Ch 7 s6, s8, s11; Ch 8 s7, s9
  1. LW-01HybridLegal counsel

    Review the draft PPP contract and qualification requirements

    Use 'PPP contract'. 'Concession' is generally reserved for user-pays contracts. Confirm powers of representatives and good standing.

    Ch 5 s7.1 pp.111-112; Gl p.9, p.34; Ch 1 p.15
  2. LW-02HybridLegal counsel

    Legal regime, regulation and framework

    Private or administrative law, procurement law, sector regulator, land, licensing, tax, and currency convertibility and repatriation.

    Ch 2 pp.33-35
  3. LW-03HybridLegal counsel

    Risk allocation clauses

    Compensation events, relief events, contractual force majeure, change in law, the all-risk clause, default and cure.

    Ch 5 s5.7 pp.96-98, p.102, s5.9 p.104, s9.3-9.4 pp.143-148, App A s1.4.1 p.194
  4. LW-04HybridLegal counsel

    Variations, change of control and refinancing consent

    Variation procedure, share lock-up and change-of-control approval, and the authority's refinancing consent and its grounds for refusal.

    Ch 8 s7.1-7.4 pp.31-33, Box 8.5 p.38, s7.8.4 p.39; Ch 7 s8.2 p.63
  5. LW-05HybridLegal counsel

    Termination and compensation on termination

    Compensation by termination type. For force majeure termination the guide's good practice is full recovery of debt, breakage and equity at an equity IRR of zero. Some contracts allow a small positive real return of around 2 percent.

For reviewers

Open questions for practitioners

The map follows the guide closely, but some choices rest on judgement. These are the ones where experienced PPP practitioners can improve it most.

  1. Is the end game in the right order for an MDB-financed bid?

    Mock evaluation, then price freeze, model audit, locked contractor prices and lender letters, then sponsor investment committees and the decision to submit (Phases 9 to 11).

  2. How much negotiation with the preferred bidder happens in practice?

    The guide calls it not desirable. AW-03 limits it to clarifications and minor changes, such as how the proposal maps onto the authority's terms.

  3. Which tasks should a model never draft, even with a person signing?

    The current split is 37 human-only, 74 hybrid and 3 model-run.

  4. Has practice moved since the 2016 guide in ways the map should reflect?

    For example on unsolicited proposals, committed finance at bid, or ESG requirements.
